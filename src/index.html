What I Do to Keep the Lights On / Work stuff

I'm a software developer going all the way back to 2005. If you're doing the math to guess my age, please stop. Trust that I'm still a young just very mature for my age.

My development career started in Youngstown, OH with Visual Basic .NET (stop laughing) and .NET WinForms. Perhaps not coincidentally, working with WinForms ran parallel with me beginning to think I wasted 4 years of my life getting a bachelor's degree...

I was in the .NET Windows development space for several years before I expanded my horizons to web development in 2012 while working in the North Hills of Pittsburgh, PA. Webforms...yeah, I definitely should have gotten that dance theory degree instead.

All joking aside, the Win/WebForms experience helps me better appreciate the modern tooling and frameworks that I've been working with for the better part of the last decade. While I'm still firmly entrenched in the .NET space, I'm as much, if not more of, a frontend developer now working primarily with React Web and React Native.

What I do for fun

Gaming

To my family's shame, I never grew out of video games. In fact, video games are what ultimately lead to my career. The story basically goes like this:

  • I liked video games
  • I built a computer for video games
  • I liked my computer
  • I liked (and still like) food and electricity
  • Idea: work with computers for food an electricity and more video games

I've been gaming a long time. I started out on an Atari 2600 (please don't do the math) and the ColecoVision, moved through all of the generations of Nintendo, Sega (except the 32x), Microsoft and Sony consoles and a few gaming rigs. I'm a console peasant now as I got out of the PC hardware rat race years ago. I still game nearly every day though, just not for 10-hours at a time anymore.
