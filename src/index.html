What I Do to Keep the Lights On or My Developer Development

I'm a software developer going all the way back to 2005. If you're doing the math to guess my age, please stop. Trust that I'm still young and spry. I'm just very mature for my age.

My development career started in Youngstown, OH with Visual Basic .NET (you read that correctly) and .NET WinForms. Perhaps not coincidentally, working with WinForms ran parallel with me developing a very strong belief that 🔥Hell🔥 may very well exist.

I was in the .NET Windows development space for several years before I expanded my horizons to web development in 2012 while working in the North Hills of Pittsburgh, PA. It was here that my certainty in the existence of Hell was solidified as I was now staring into the face of true evil: .NET WebForms

All joking aside, the severe, tortuous pain of the WinForms and WebForms experience helped me better appreciate the modern tooling and frameworks that I've been working with for the better part of the last decade. While I'm still firmly entrenched in the .NET/Azure space, I'm more of a frontend developer now, working primarily with React Web and React Native.

What I do for fun

Gaming

Much to my family's shame, I did not grow out of video games. In fact, video games are what ultimately lead to my career. It basically went like this:

  • I like video games
  • I build computer for video games
  • I like computer
  • I like food and electricity
  • I get job where I can use computer to get food and electricity

I started out gaming on an Atari 2600 (please don't do the math) and the ColecoVision, moved through all of the generations of Nintendo, Sega (except the 32x), Microsoft and Sony consoles and custom PCs. I'm only a console peasant now as I got out of the PC hardware rat race years ago. I still game nearly every day, just not for 10-hour sessions anymore.
